4\. Finding an Accountant

## Step 1) Treat finance like “risk management,” not admin

Goal: stay out of trouble and keep more of what you earn.

- Getting it wrong = penalties, audits, stress, and in worst cases legal issues.
- Getting it right = you sleep at night + your business actually compounds.

Action: Set your “non-negotiables” for money ops today:

- Create a simple checklist: “Invoices numbered + compliant, receipts saved, taxes reserved, deadlines tracked, accountant on speed dial.”
- Block 30 mins every Friday for a money admin sweep (invoices sent, expenses tagged, tax pot topped up).

---

## Step 2) Hire an accountant like you’re hiring a co-pilot (not a box-ticker)

Goal: stop guessing and build a setup that fits your exact situation.

- Find someone who specializes in contractors/freelancers/small businesses (not big-company accountants).
- They must understand your jurisdiction rules (UK: IR35, VAT thresholds, LTD rules / US: 1099 context, LLC→S-Corp, state-by-state tax quirks).

Action: Use this mini-vetting script on 3 accountants:

- “How many people like "me" do you work with?”
- “What’s the most tax-efficient structure for my income range in my state/country?”
- “Do you support Xero/QuickBooks and will you access my books directly?”
- “What do you need from me monthly to keep everything clean?”

---

## Step 3) Set up the right business structure (and upgrade it when it makes sense)

Goal: don’t overpay tax because you picked the wrong setup.

- UK: LTD company is usually the most efficient for consultants; learn salary/dividend basics.
- US: often start LLC, then move to S-Corp when income justifies the extra admin (reasonable salary + distributions).

Action: Ask your accountant for a “structure roadmap”:

- “At what annual profit does it make sense to switch to S-Corp (or change approach)?”
- “What salary/distribution split is considered reasonable for my niche?”
- Get the answer in writing (even an email) so you can refer back later.

---

## Step 4) Run everything through cloud accounting (no exceptions)

Goal: automate the boring stuff so you don’t miss payments or deadlines.

- Cloud tools handle invoicing, bank syncing, and make tax prep way easier.
- Your accountant can log in and sort things faster—but you still must categorize correctly.

Action: Pick ONE platform and wire it up properly:

- Choose Xero or QuickBooks (ask your accountant which they prefer / include).
- Connect business bank + cards.
- Create rules/categories (software, travel, meals, hardware, subcontractors, etc.).
- Turn on reminders for invoice follow-ups and tax dates.

---

## Step 5) Get paid cleanly: compliant invoices + ruthless follow-up

Goal: protect cash flow and avoid compliance headaches.

- Invoices need correct numbering + required details (region-specific) or you can get penalized in an audit.
- Payment terms matter: net 15/30 (sometimes 60 for big firms), and late terms should exist in your contract.

Action: Build your “default invoice system” once:

- Create one invoice template with: invoice number, tax info, your business details, payment method, net terms.
- Add an automated follow-up sequence: reminder at day 3, day 7, day 14 past due.
- If you do projects/value pricing: set milestone invoices (e.g., 50% upfront / 50% on completion) and send immediately.

---

## Step 6) Track expenses like a pro (and separate business from personal)

Goal: claim what you’re allowed to claim, without getting wrecked in an audit.

- Separate business and personal spending—mixing them creates chaos and missed deductions.
- Keep receipts for years (UK often ~5; US can be longer—keep 7–10 to be safe).

Action: Build a “receipts + expenses” habit that takes 60 seconds:

- Use a business bank account + card only for business purchases.
- Snap a photo of every receipt and upload it immediately to cloud storage (folder by month).
- Expense categories to review: software, hardware, home office, professional development, travel, professional services (legal/accounting/subcontractors).

---

## Step 7) Pay yourself smart + protect cash flow with “tax pots” and an emergency fund

Goal: stop accidentally spending tax money and avoid panic during dry spells.

- “Money in the business” isn’t all yours—tax is coming (often later), and it can hit hard.
- Best practice: separate accounts for (1) operating cash, (2) tax savings, (3) emergency fund.

Action: Implement the 3-account rule this week:

*Open/label accounts:* \*Operating\*\*, **Tax**, **Emergency**.

- Every time you pay yourself (salary/dividend/distribution), also move a % into the Tax account (use a rough estimate your accountant confirms).
- Build an emergency fund: minimum 3 months, ideal 6 months (separate from tax). Don’t touch it unless it’s a real emergency.
