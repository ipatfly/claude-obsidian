What is Vibe Coding?

\# 1.1 — What Vibe Coding Actually Is (And Isn't)

## Key Summary

Vibe coding isn't "talk to AI and magic happens." It started when Andrej Karpathy — ex-Tesla, ex-OpenAI — tweeted about how he just "vibes with the AI and code appears." The term went viral, but most people took the wrong lesson from it.

Here's the truth: AI doesn't think. It's a text prediction engine in a trench coat. It matches patterns and predicts the next word — that's it. And when you understand that, everything changes. Because if your AI is 90% accurate per step, by step 10 you're down to 35% accuracy. Errors compound fast.

So vibe coding isn't typing "make me an app" and walking away. It's directing AI with natural language while YOU handle the architecture, the quality control, and the judgment calls. Think of it as a 4-step loop: **Direct → Generate → Review → Refine.** Each cycle gets you closer to exactly what you want.

The old model was writing every line of code yourself. The new model is being the editor-in-chief — reviewing, directing, and refining what AI generates. Your domain expertise (sales, marketing, ops, whatever you've spent years doing) is now your unfair advantage because AI can write code, but it can't understand YOUR business the way you do.

\---

## Actionable Takeaways

1\. **Stop treating AI like a magic wand.** It's a prediction machine, not a thinking partner. When you accept that, you'll start giving it better instructions instead of blaming it for bad output.

2\. **Think "Editor-in-Chief," not "developer."** Your job isn't to write code — it's to define clearly what you want, give it the right context, review what comes back, and push back when it's wrong. You already do this in your day job with people. Same skill, different tool.

3\. **Context is everything.** If you can't describe what you want clearly enough for another human to build it, AI won't get it either. Before your next prompt, ask yourself: "Would a smart contractor know exactly what to do with this brief?"

4\. **Don't accept "close enough."** If a button doesn't work the way you want, say so. If the design is off, describe what's wrong. The people who fail at vibe coding are the ones who give up and accept mediocre output instead of refining it.

5\. **Your domain expertise is your superpower.** You don't need to become a developer. You need to combine what you already know (your industry, your clients, your problems) with the frameworks in this course. That's what makes your builds better than someone with zero context just prompting randomly.
